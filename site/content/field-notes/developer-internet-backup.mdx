Developers think in redundancy: load balancers, replicas, fallbacks. Travel connectivity deserves the same mindset. One Wi‑Fi password is not a plan.

This guide explains how to design a **simple backup stack** for work on the road-especially in India, where coverage and hotel infrastructure vary sharply by region.

## Why one connection is not enough

Accommodation Wi‑Fi can be:

- Fast at check-in and unusable during peak hours.
- Shared across dozens of devices on one access point.
- Blocked or filtered for VPN or non-standard ports.
- Down during power cuts with no UPS on the router.

A phone hotspot is not automatic insurance either-indoor signal, data caps, and thermal throttling on long video calls all matter.

## The three-layer model

### Layer 1 - Primary (fixed location)

Use hotel, homestay, or coworking Ethernet/Wi‑Fi when you are stationary for focused work.

**Before booking**, ask the host (in writing when possible):

- Typical download/upload speeds.
- Whether video calls work from the room you will use.
- Power backup for the router or property.
- Whether a desk or quiet corner is available.

<Callout title="General recommendation">
Run a speed test from the **same room** you plan to work in-not only from the lobby.
</Callout>

### Layer 2 - Personal hotspot

Your phone (or a dedicated hotspot device) is the usual backup.

Practical tips:

- Confirm your data plan allows tethering and enough volume for your week.
- On dual-SIM phones, pick the carrier with better coverage at the destination-not the one that works in your home city.
- Keep the phone charged; hotspot drains battery quickly.
- For long calls, prefer Wi‑Fi calling or a wired headset to reduce radio heat and dropouts.

### Layer 3 - Optional second path

Add a third option only when the trip justifies cost and weight:

- Second SIM or eSIM on a different network.
- Travel router that accepts SIM or bridges hotel login to your devices.
- Known coworking space or cafe with tested uplink (have a offline fallback if you rely on this).

## Test before the critical moment

Schedule a **connectivity rehearsal** before important work:

1. Join a test video call from the primary network.
2. Switch to hotspot mid-call and confirm recovery time.
3. Verify VPN, SSH, and git push over each path.
4. Note upload speed-deployments and screen sharing need headroom.

If a path fails, fix or replan **before** the client demo-not ten minutes before it starts.

## When the internet fails during work

Have a written runbook:

- **Meetings:** Switch to audio-only or reschedule with a template message ready.
- **Git:** Work locally; push when any path returns.
- **Deploys:** Delay unless you have confirmed uplink and rollback plan.
- **Support:** Notify stakeholders with ETA once you know which layer is available.

Link this habit back to the pillar guide: [Working remotely while traveling](/field-notes/nomad-remote-work).

## India-specific considerations

- **Remote valleys and passes:** Treat cellular maps as hints; ask locals or recent travelers when possible.
- **Monsoon:** Power and backhaul outages cluster-keep offline tasks queued.
- **Rail journeys:** Coverage is intermittent; download tickets and work offline for long segments.

## Quick checklist

<Checklist title="Connectivity prep">

- Primary network tested from your workspace.
- Hotspot tested with realistic data use.
- VPN and git verified on both paths.
- Stakeholders know your timezone and response windows.
- Offline tasks ready if all layers fail.

</Checklist>
